Buddy Cave Exploring

Four clans of buddies walk the same real cave at the same moment. Three try the rules from the brainstorm; the fourth walks the design decided after watching them: survey up close, then patrol. The floors are the server's own walking mesh, cut to one-yard squares. Pick a cave, let them run, and watch how each one works through it.

"tunnels may curve. A 20 yard line isn't necessarily indicative of a doorway. I'm not sure about a solution, wanna take a break from questions and do some brainstorming?" (the owner, 2026-10-07)

Today's roaming finds tunnels by standing at an area's middle and looking for a direction where the view suddenly reaches 20 yards farther. A curving tunnel bends out of sight, and an open field gives long views everywhere, so the test both misses tunnels and finds false ones. Every rule here measures by walking, not by straight lines.

The rules

Every walk below is found by the same path finder: the cheapest way on foot over the floor's squares, a little dearer within 2½ yards of a wall, so buddies keep off walls where there is room. The rules differ only in where they send a buddy next. Each diagram is the same small cave: a room by the entrance, a curving tunnel, and a side branch off it.

Walking rings

How it works
Water poured at the entrance spreads along the floor. Every square is stamped with how far the water walked to reach it. Sorting every square by that walk gives a list from nearest to farthest. Your Y rule (1 to 100, moved 1 to 20 at each waypoint) picks a place in the list, and the waypoint is a random square near that place.
Why tunnels come for free
The water follows every bend, so a curving tunnel is just more squares in the list. Every percent of the list holds the same number of squares, so waypoints fall evenly over the floor: the even wash.
What it costs the server
One flood of the area, done ahead of time like the town bakes. Choosing a waypoint is a single lookup.
Watch for
Long trips. One ring of a branching cave lies in every branch at once, so a buddy may cross the whole cave between two waypoints.

Unseen edge

How it works
The clan remembers every square it has seen, with sight stopped by walls out to 40 yards, and forgets after 4 minutes. A buddy walks to the nearest spot, on foot, where remembered floor meets floor nobody remembers, skipping any spot within 12 yards of another buddy's goal.
Why tunnels come for free
A curving tunnel shows itself one bend at a time. Each step in uncovers the next unseen edge, so the buddy follows the curve.
What it costs the server
A memory grid per clan (617e6's paint grid already is one), sight lines a few times a second, and a search outward from the buddy each time it picks a goal.
Watch for
Once everything is seen, nothing pulls the buddies anywhere until memories fade. Then they go back to whatever was seen longest ago.

Floor skeleton

How it works
The floor is shaved from its edges inward until a one-square line runs down the middle of every passage. Its forks are junctions, its tips dead ends, the lines between them passages. Forks closer than 3 yards are one fork, and twigs under 6 yards are cut off. Buddies follow Trémaux's rule for mazes, kept by the whole clan: take a passage nobody has set off along; if there is none, go back the way you came; when every passage has been walked there and back, start a new round.
Why tunnels come for free
Every tunnel is a branch of the skeleton, curved or not, and the rule walks every branch to its end.
What it costs the server
The skeleton is built once per area ahead of time. Walking it is a short list of passages with a mark on each.
Watch for
Buddies keep to the middle line, so the corners of a wide cavern are seen only from that line, and a large hall takes a while.

Survey, then patrol

Surveying
The unseen edge, with one change: floor counts as explored only once a buddy has had it in view within 30 yards (closer than the 40-yard sight), so side rooms get walked up to, not glanced at from across a hall. Nothing is forgotten while any floor is left unexplored. Each buddy takes the nearest edge on foot that is not within 12 yards of another buddy's goal.
Patrolling
Once the floor is all explored, the clan builds one loop. The watchman stops are picked greedily (each time, the stop that sees the most floor not yet seen from a stop) until 98% of the floor is in view of some stop. The loop through them is the nearest-next tour by walking distance, with crossed legs uncrossed. Each buddy joins the loop at the stop nearest where its survey ended and walks it a random way round, glancing 40 yards at each step. A buddy about to head for the stop another is already heading for skips past it or turns round, so they stay spread like runners on a track.
What it costs the server
Looking is shadowcasting (each square in view visited once), and only after walking 2 yards, never in a fight. The loop is built once per area.
Watch for
The moment the survey ends, the loop of watchman stops is drawn. In a big dungeon the loop is long: Wailing Caverns' has dozens of stops.

Decided so far

  • The unseen edgewas the owner's pick after watching the first three ("Unseen edge wins!").
  • Explored means close"gotta get closer than 40 yards ... how will you know if there's any secret passages". Surveying first used 5 yards, which took three buddies about an hour in Wailing Caverns; the owner then set it to 30 ("survey-then-patrol needs a larger viewing distance. Let's say 30 yards"). The 40-yard glance is for patrolling, to see into a room without walking in.
  • No forgetting clockwhile anything is unexplored. A big dungeon with fights still has its edges when the fight ends.
  • Patrol, don't settle"keep patrolling because monsters respawn". The watchman loop is built only after the survey, otherwise every buddy would head for the same best stop.
  • One loop, everyone cycleseach buddy joins where its survey ended; catching up means skipping past or turning round.
  • Fights firsta monster found while surveying or patrolling is fought at once, and the walk is planned again after. (This page has no monsters.)
  • Spread out in the open world, together in instancesinstances have no patrol; an ally's fight is joined only within one's own section of the instance.
  • A changed area(a door opens, as in Shadowfang Keep): only the new floor is surveyed.
  • Still openwhy buddies would backpedal and strafe; where a split instance's section edges lie.

Where the floors come from

The server plans every creature's walk on its navigation mesh, a map of each spot a character can stand, cut into small flat pieces. scripts/generate-exploration-floors reads those pieces for a box of the world and lays them onto one-yard squares. It keeps the largest stretch of floor that lies under the ground (the terrain is at least 6 yards higher above it), plus 30 yards outside each entrance. Behind it is the game's own outdoor minimap. Wailing Caverns is a dungeon with no terrain at all, so its whole floor counts, and its buddies start where the game puts a player who walks in.

Two things are flattened. Where one passage runs over another, the lower floor is drawn and the two share the squares. And Wailing Caverns is drawn at two yards a square so the browser keeps up.
