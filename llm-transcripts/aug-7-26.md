Not absurd — and ALE gives us more than you'd need. `CreatePacket` and a full
`WorldPacket` class exist, plus `Player:SendPacket` and a `GetItemLink` global.
So all versions of this are buildable.


But your model of the failure is slightly off in a way that changes the design.
**The client isn't failing to get the data. It's getting it too late and never
redrawing.**


When an item entry the client doesn't know shows up, the client *does*
automatically fire `CMSG_ITEM_QUERY_SINGLE` and the server answers. That happens
on its own. The problem is that your character model is built at world-enter
from whatever display IDs are on hand at that instant — which is none — and
when the responses land a few hundred milliseconds later, nothing invalidates
the already-drawn model. The data arrives; the render already happened.


That's why the manual fix worked and why it had to be manual: unequip/re-equip
does *two* things, and only the second one is the actual repair. By the time you
dragged the item, the cache was already warm from the automatic query.
Re-equipping just forced a model rebuild.


So the bug has two halves, and any fix needs both:


**Half one — warm the cache.** Three ways, cheapest first:

- **Item links.** `GetItemLink(entry)` into a chat message the player never
  really sees. Rendering a link requires the template, so the client queries on
  miss. A handful of lines, no bag space, no inventory churn. This is the
  standard private-server trick.

- **Your inventory cycle.** Works, but each item has to survive at least one
  update tick or the object create/destroy may coalesce and the client never
  sees it — so it's not a tight loop, it's 51 items each needing a beat. And
  51 items won't fit in a backpack at once.

- **Hand-built query responses.** `CreatePacket` an
  `SMSG_ITEM_QUERY_SINGLE_RESPONSE` per entry and push it unprompted. No round
  trip at all, fully deterministic — but that packet is ~130 fields in exact
  order, and one wrong field corrupts everything after it.


**Half two — force the redraw.** Warming alone changes nothing on a model
that's already on screen. You still need to dirty the visible-item fields, which
is what the equip cycle actually accomplishes.


Here's what makes me not want it as the fix for the kit, though. `itemcache.wdb`
is **per client installation and persists on disk** — not per character. Which
means the thing you'd be compensating for isn't a property of the character at
all, and `PLAYER_EVENT_ON_FIRST_LOGIN` would be the wrong hook: it fires once
per character, while a player who wipes their WDB, or logs in from a second
machine, would be right back to question marks on a character that already used
up its one firing. To be safe it has to run every login, forever, on all 51
entries, to compensate for using entry numbers no client knows — when the
entry numbers every client already knows are free.


**Where this absolutely is the right answer: issue 405's Ability Tomes.** Those
are entries 900001+, genuinely new items with no canonical original to fall back
on. There's no in-place option there. The warming mechanism isn't a workaround
for tomes, it's the only way they'll ever render — and 405 is currently
written as though that problem doesn't exist.


So my recommendation is: keep the revert to original IDs for the kit, and build
this as a general "make the client aware of a custom item" utility aimed at 405,
where it earns its keep.


One thing I'd like your read on, since it's the part I'm least sure of. Given
