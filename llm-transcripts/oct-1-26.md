## Where they intersect

  AzerothCore's world (what this project needs), and it will have a "WC3 map
  mode" (what world-edit-to-execute needs). Its file-reading layer is one shared
  C library that both sides call; world-edit-to-execute calls it from LuaJIT.

  world-edit-to-execute's WoW-client phase as its "Stage A":
  1. Read the client's archives, tables, textures and models.
  2. Draw those models.
  3. Use a capture rig that films the real client and our client showing the

  4. Run the "asset forge": find an openly licensed model or generate one, fit

  the stock client as a stopgap.
- **The same model formats at the boundary.** This project keeps models in glTF,

## The shape you described, and how it differs from the plan as written

1. **Votes and the similarity score are two separate gates.** Gallery votes pick
   which candidate people like. The score only says whether that candidate is
   far enough from Blizzard's original. A model has to pass both.
2. **The score isn't one classifier that marks parts of an image as similar.**
   It combines four measurements into a single number, where 0 means identical
   and 1 means unrelated:
   - **Outline:** both models are rendered from a fixed set of 10 cameras (8
     around, 1 above, 1 in front at eye height), and the score measures how
     little their filled outlines overlap.
   - **3D shape:** about 10,000 points are sampled on each surface and compared
     directly in 3D. No pictures are involved in this part.
   - **Colour:** the colour histograms of the matching renders are compared.
   - **Overall look:** a vision model (DINOv2 or CLIP) turns each render into a
     vector, and the score measures how far apart the vectors are. This catches
     style and detail the other three miss.

   There are no varied backgrounds. Both models are scaled to the same height
   and posed in the same frame of the same animation. The ring of 40 views (8
   directions at 1, 3 or 5 heights) is a different thing: it's how the generator
   builds a model, not how the score judges it.
3. **There is no threshold yet,** neither 30% nor 50%. The plan is to score a
   few examples you've judged by eye and set the number from those.
4. **The score is written down as explicitly *not* a legal test.** A model made
   by feeding a render of Blizzard's original into image-to-3D starts from the
   original. It can keep recognisable design features even with a high score.
   That's why every model also records how it was made: "derived" (made from or
   while looking at the original) or "independent." The independent route is the
   clean-room loop: describe the unit in words, build from the description,
   check the result. That record is the stronger evidence. The distance number
   backs it up.
5. **The score is never shown to whatever builds the next candidate.** Your idea
   of spending credits on "the most similar" model is still consistent with
   that. Choosing *which* model to rework is fine; the rule only forbids
   steering the generator with the number.

**Is it what we want?** With your new 1.0 goal (our client, their art), the
score stops being what gates 1.0. It becomes the gate for the full version you'd
sell. For that version, I'd put "independent lineage, plus enough distance"
ahead of distance alone.

There's a harder problem too: the art isn't the only thing that came from
Blizzard. The quests, NPC text and spell data come from AzerothCore's database,
which was taken from Blizzard's game. No model score touches that.

That leads to the question I'd most like your answer to: **if the sold version
must be legally distinct, which parts of the game besides the art need their own
"replaced" measure?**

## Your question: is the threshold legally defined?

No. US copyright law has no percentage. Copying is judged by "substantial
similarity": whether an ordinary observer would see the protected expression as
taken, or whether the protected elements match when compared piece by piece.
It's a judgment, not a number. The "change 30% and it's yours" rule is a myth.
Copying a small part that is central to a work can still infringe. So the
threshold is ours to choose, and the plan stays: score a few examples you judge
by eye and set the number from those. I'm not a lawyer, so treat this as
background, not advice.

palette: a list of colours, each three bytes of red, green and blue. Recolouring
