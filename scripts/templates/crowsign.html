Crowsign

Sprite-craft: enchanting as the gathering profession of magic, and two ways to build it without touching the game client. The design is the owner's, from the conversation of 26 September 2026 (kept whole in notes/vision-enchanting-system-update.md, "Second Conversation"). Nothing here is built or scheduled. Every list and count on this page is read from the client's data files and the world database by scripts/generate-crowsign-page; the prose is written by hand.

1. Crowsign, the design

"can we make a secondary profession? If no, then enchanting should have the ability to "investigate" the natural world via herbs and leathers and hides. these could be imbued into "sprites", which are floating elementals of fist-to-watermelon size. if so, then that could be the profession - crowsign."

"They can be enchanted with upgrades, and they apply them as small HoT effects. Or similar, the fire one can light foes on fire - minor DoT, just enough to feel spiny. And you can have up to four, of any color."

"we can of course enchant these onto vellums, stories and tales given to the spirit world."

"return from your trial with a powerful vial, when drank it will give you a powerful sword."

"(doesn't create items, that's conjuration. Instead, it applies magical effects to weapons and armor like... y'know, enchantments)"

Read as a whole, the idea makes enchanting a gathering profession. Enchanting takes magic out of two places. From gear it takes dust, essences, shards and crystals, which is what Disenchant does today. From the natural world (herbs, leathers, hides) it would take sprites, through a new action the owner calls "investigate". Then it passes that magic on to the other crafts. Crowsign is the name of the sprite half. It becomes its own secondary profession if the game allows one. If not, it stays inside enchanting as the "investigate" action.

Where the magic goes

in the stock game the owner's idea offered as an alternative, not decided shards, crystals, gems dust sprites

Sprite rules, as said

  • What a sprite is: "a floating elemental of fist-to-watermelon size", carried as an inventory item.
  • How one is made: a profession spell creates it, "like a runed copper rod" (Enchanting's first recipe, which turns a copper rod, a Strange Dust and a Lesser Magic Essence into the rod every enchantment needs).
  • What goes in: what "investigate" draws out of herbs, leathers and hides.
  • What it does: small effects over time. A heal over time, or for the fire sprite a minor burn on foes, "just enough to feel spiny".
  • How many: "up to four, of any color".
  • How it grows: "they can be enchanted with upgrades".
  • What it is not: conjuration. Enchanting "applies magical effects to weapons and armor", and so to sprites. It does not make swords out of vials.

The other flows, as said

  • Gems: "level determines purity and size, colour determines magical effects when crushed into enchantments". Enchanting could enchant dusts and crystals. An arcane crystal would be something like a Large Glimmering Shard, "a token of raw magic infused into a mineralized [glass]". It would then be given to jewelcrafters.
  • Alternative: enchantments could require cut gems instead of large glimmering shards, and large glimmering shards (from disenchanted weapons) would go to jewelcrafters.
  • Dust: to blacksmiths ("incredibly dense minerals"), leatherworkers ("paints that never go dry and adhere to the leather") and tailors ("dyes and processing steps, but pronounced sops, that modified the threads and gave them enchantments").

The stock game already has two of these flows in miniature. Enchanting makes Enchanted Thorium at skill 250, which use. It also makes Enchanted Leather at skill 250, which use. The materials table in section 4 counts which crafts consume each dust and shard today.

2. Route A: Crowsign as part of Enchanting ("investigate")

This is the route the owner named as the fallback: "an additional ability to enchanting if we can't make our own new profession". It needs no new skill. Enchanting is skill line 333, with a recipe window, a skill bar from 1 to 450, and spells the client already lists under it. Crowsign would live inside that window and grow the Enchanting skill.

What the client decides, and what the server decides

The client knows every spell's name, icon, tooltip, reagent list and cast bar from its own spell file. It also builds the recipe window from its own skill-line file: which spells appear under Enchanting, and at which skill they turn yellow, green and grey. The server only confirms what is cast. None of that can change without a client patch.

The server owns the rest. It decides what a spell does when cast, through its copy of the spell file, which it can override row by row in the spell_dbc table ( rows today, all stock). It owns every item in item_template: an item's name, stats, icon (any icon the client has), stack size and flags reach the client from the server. It owns loot tables, trainers, and scripts (C++ spell scripts, and Lua through the ALE engine). Every client data file has a matching server-side override table. skillline_dbc, skilllineability_dbc and skillraceclassinfo_dbc exist and are empty. They change only the server's picture, never what the client shows.

So Route A has one rule. Every Crowsign action borrows a spell the client already lists under Enchanting, or one the client will let the player cast. The client keeps that spell's name, icon and listed reagents. The server changes what it does.

How the three "break it down" spells work today

Investigate is "equivalent to disenchant". The game already has three spells of that kind: Disenchant (Enchanting), Prospecting (Jewelcrafting, ore into gems) and Milling (Inscription, herbs into pigments). All three follow one path through the server (Spell.cpp, the cast check; SpellEffects.cpp, the effect). First the cast check tests the targeted item. Then the effect opens a loot window of a special kind. The loot comes from a table keyed by the item, and the item is used up when the loot is taken.

Investigate: three ways to build it

1. Disenchant does both

One button, as the owner put it. Disenchant stays the spell. The server's cast check lets it through for herbs, leathers and hides as well as gear, and the effect sends those items to a loot table of Crowsign rows. That takes a small reversible C++ patch (a patches/B*.sh), or a spell script bound to spell 13262 that replaces the cast check and the effect.

Unknown: whether the client itself refuses to aim Disenchant at a herb before asking the server. One test in game settles it.

2. Rows in a table no herb uses

Loot is keyed by item id. Investigate's loot could sit in prospecting_loot_template under herb and leather ids: no ore shares those ids, so nothing collides. The effect would open a prospecting-type loot window, so the consume-five-on-loot step already works. Only the check (which items, which skill) needs new code.

3. Lua only, no loot window

An ALE hook on spell cast watches for the chosen spell and its target item. It removes the herbs and hands over the sprite material directly, with no C++. It is the quickest to try. The drawback is that the player gets no loot window, and the cast has to be allowed through first, so this still meets the client question in 1.

The flags and ranks that decide what can be investigated are all item fields the server owns. Reuse RequiredSkillRank as the Enchanting skill needed, the way prospecting and milling use it. Mark each investigable item in the loot table, not with a flag: the two free flag bits belong to Jewelcrafting and Inscription. The server's world database has trade goods in the three groups investigate could take. They are listed below, with whether each is already milled and in how many skinning loot groups it appears (one group can serve many creatures). skill_discovery_template ( rows) is the stock mechanism for "sometimes you discover a new recipe while crafting", which fits "investigate" too.

The elemental group already holds the game's own coloured magic in item form: Elemental Fire, Essence of Water, the Outland motes, the Northrend crystallized elements. These are the closest things the client has to sprite essences, with names and icons it already knows.

Making sprites: which recipe slot

A sprite is an item, and new items are possible: the server supplies the name, stats and icon. (The vision note records one caveat. Whether a brand-new item id shows its icon everywhere in this client is untested. The Outland ability tomes, 155q, will show it.) The recipe that makes the sprite is the hard part. To appear in the Enchanting window it must be a spell the client already lists under Enchanting. The window then shows that spell's client-side name, reagents and tool, and the item id the client thinks it creates. That item's name and icon come from the server.

So there are three ways to get a sprite recipe into the window:

  1. Take over a product. Choose an Enchanting recipe whose product the game can spare and rewrite that product's item row as a sprite. The window then shows, for example, "Lesser Magic Wand", and the item it makes appears as the sprite, with the sprite's name and icon. The recipe name stays wrong, but everything else lines up, and it needs no spell override at all. The wands are the plainest candidates (levels 10 to 175 of skill).
  2. Change what a recipe makes. Override the recipe's created item in spell_dbc. The bag gets a sprite, but the window still shows the old product. This is the only use for the recipes the client lists under Enchanting that no trainer or recipe item teaches (the retired TBC "Arcane Dust" recipe, 28021, is one).
  3. No window. Craft sprites from a menu, either gossip on an item such as the rod or on a trainer, written in Lua. Any names are possible, and the skill still rises through the server. There is no recipe window and no skill colours.

Vellums: in 3.3.5 every enchantment recipe can also be cast onto a vellum to make a scroll, which is its own item that the server owns. Enchanting recipes name a scroll this way. Scrolls are the natural home for "stories and tales given to the spirit world", and for carrying an upgrade to a sprite. Using the scroll on a sprite could apply the enchantment server-side, whatever the enchant spell's own target rules say.

Carrying sprites, and what they do

Four sprites, four bag slots

The character sheet has exactly four bag slots. The server applies an equipped item's stats, its "on equip" spells and its enchantments to every slot up to and including the bags. In Player.cpp, the item-modifier step runs for slots 0 to 22, and 19 to 22 are the four bags. A sprite made as a small bag with an on-equip aura would give "up to four, of any colour" with no new code, and enchanting a sprite would be an ordinary item enchantment. The cost is bag space, unless sprites carry a few slots of their own. Read in the source, not yet tried in game.

Carried loose in the bags

"On equip" spells do not run for items sitting inside bags. A Lua script would count sprite items when the bags change (or on a timer) and apply or remove the matching auras, capped at four. This means more code, and the four-limit is a rule the script enforces rather than a shape the game already has.

Seen floating

"Floating elementals of fist-to-watermelon size" can also be seen. The server can spawn small creatures that follow the player and circle at a radius. The project's orbit maths is already written for monsters. Any creature model the client has can be used; the creature list in section 4 has the wisps, motes, sprites and faeries. Only one companion pet can be out at a time, so four sprites would be server-spawned followers, not pets.

The effects are auras. The buff bar shows the aura's client-side name and icon, so each colour of sprite borrows an existing spell whose name fits and whose effect kind is right: heal over time, damage over time, or a damage shield. A damage shield is the stock game's "spiny": it hurts whoever strikes you, like Thorns, which is exactly "just enough to feel spiny". The server sets the numbers through spell_dbc, so small stays small. Candidates are in section 3's sprite-name list: filter by "heal over time" or "damage over time", and prefer spells that no player learns ("NPC only"), so no class ability is changed by accident.

Every Enchanting spell, by the skill it is learned at

All spells the client lists under Enchanting (skill line 333), with the lowest skill any trainer or recipe item asks for. "With the skill" means it comes free when Enchanting is learned. "None" means nothing in this database teaches it. Yellow and grey are the client's own colour points; green falls halfway between them. Trainers teach the ranks by character level: Artisan (to 300) at 35, Master (to 375) at 50, Grand Master (to 450) at 65. Basic (level cap 60) therefore reaches skill 375, vanilla (40) reaches 300, and beta (20) reaches 225 (Expert is taught at 20).

3. Route B: Crowsign as its own skill

What a real new profession needs

A profession is a skill line. For the client to show one, its own data files need all of the following:

  • a row in SkillLine: the name ("Crowsign"), the heading it sits under (Professions or Secondary Skills) and its icon;
  • rows in SkillRaceClassInfo: who may hold it, and whether the skills panel draws a bar;
  • a rank row in SkillTiers: the 75 / 150 / 225 / 300 / 375 / 450 steps;
  • one row in SkillLineAbility per recipe: the list the recipe window shows, and each recipe's yellow and grey points;
  • a row in Spell for the "open the window" spell and for every recipe: name, icon, reagents, cast time and tooltip.

The client reads these from its archives. Adding them means shipping a patch archive to every player, and this project makes no client patches. The server-side copies (skillline_dbc and the rest) would make the server accept a skill 800 called Crowsign. The client would still not know its name, would draw nothing, and would open no window. A new skill line is not possible now.

What is possible: take over a skill line the client already has and nobody uses. The client keeps its name and icon, and its recipe window (if any) lists only the spells the client already files under it. The server decides who holds it, its value and maximum, how it rises, and what its spells do.

Every skill line a player could see

These are every skill line under Professions, Secondary Skills, Languages and Not Displayed, plus the three class-skill lines that behave like professions. "Bar" comes from the client's own display flags: 0x400 means the client draws a flat value with no progress bar. "Window" names the spell that opens a recipe window for that line. "Max" is the highest rank in the client's tier file, or "by level" when the line has no tiers (then the server's default is five points per level). The server can set any value and maximum it likes.

Survival (142): the best host

It sits under Secondary Skills, beside Cooking, First Aid and Fishing, which is where the owner asked for Crowsign ("can we make a secondary profession?"). Its display flags are 0, so the client draws a real progress bar. Every race and class may hold it. Nobody teaches it, and no item needs it. It was a secondary profession cut before release, and its one spell is Basic Campfire, which Cooking also teaches. The name reads "Survival", which suits investigating the natural world. It has no recipe window, so Crowsign's crafts would come from a Lua menu or from Enchanting's window (Route A's recipe slots), while the Survival bar grows.

Internal (769): a window, but a test bench

The only unused line with a recipe window. Internal Knowledge (36356) opens it, and the client files 35 developers' test spells under it. The server could rewrite what each one does. The window would still list them by their client names: "Uber Heal Over Time", "Master Buff (Magic)", "Death Touch", "dmg4". It sits under Class Skills with no bar. It works, but it reads like a test bench.

The retired riding skills (eight)

Horse, Wolf, Tiger, Ram, Raptor and Kodo Riding, Mechanostrider Piloting and Undead Horsemanship. Riding (762) replaced them in patch 2.0. They are under Secondary Skills, but their flags (0x492) show a flat value, not a bar, and old mount items still name them. Their names say "riding", so they make a poor fit.

Not free

Lockpicking (rogues), Runeforging (death knights), the professions, the racial lines and the languages in use. The NPC-only languages (Titan, Draconic, Old Tongue, Demon Tongue) are free but read as languages. Swimming has no race/class row, so the server deletes it at login unless a server-side row is added.

What a player would see under Route B, with Survival: "Survival 37 / 300" under Secondary Skills; the Crowsign spells in the spellbook under their borrowed client names and icons; sprite items under their new server names. The word "Crowsign" appears only where the server writes text: item names and tooltips, menus, chat. The skills panel always reads "Survival".

Client spells whose names fit sprites

Under either route, a no-patch Crowsign acts through spells the client already has. These are all spells whose names contain Sprite, Wisp, Mote, Spirit, Elemental, Faerie, Fairy, Will-o, Essence, Pixie or Firefly, sorted by spell level. "Kind" sorts them by what they could lend a sprite: a summon (a creature or object appears), a heal or damage over time, a visual or dummy aura (a server script decides what it does), or a buff. "Who has it" says whether any player skill line or trainer carries the spell (). The rest are NPC spells, and those are the safest to rewrite. "Now does" is the client's own tooltip with its numbers filled in, or the effect list when there is no tooltip.

Server-side tables a no-patch build would write

The *_dbc tables override the server's copy of a client data file row by row. spell_linked_spell makes one spell cast or apply another. spell_script_names binds a C++ spell script to a spell id. Nothing here reaches the client.

6. Open questions

  1. Which route? Crowsign as Enchanting's "investigate" (enchanters only), or as a borrowed secondary skill (Survival) that anyone can hold?
  2. How much does investigate take? One item, like Disenchant, or five from a stack, like Milling and Prospecting?
  3. Which items? Herbs, leathers and hides as said. Should cloth, meat, or the existing elemental items (Elemental Fire, the motes) be investigated as well, or be the output of investigating?
  4. What comes out? A sprite directly, or a material (an "essence of the natural world") that a recipe then shapes into a sprite "like a runed copper rod"?
  5. Where do the four sprites live? In the four bag slots (the game already applies their effects there), or loose in the bags with a script enforcing the four?
  6. "Of any colour": may all four be the same colour (four fire sprites), or one of each?
  7. Always on, or on a trigger? A passive heal over time, and for fire either a burn on nearby foes or a damage shield ("spiny") on whoever strikes you?
  8. Are sprites seen? Floating in the world, fist to watermelon by level, or only as icons in the bags?
  9. How does a sprite grow? Through enchantments laid on it like armour, or through recipes that turn a small sprite into a larger one?
  10. Gems: should enchantments ask for cut gems (the alternative offered) or keep the large shards, with the shards going on to jewelcrafters?
  11. The dust flows: reuse the stock recipes that already exist (Enchanted Thorium for blacksmiths, Enchanted Leather for leatherworkers), or borrow other recipe slots for "dense minerals", "paints that never dry" and thread "sops"?
  12. The vial line: "return from your trial with a powerful vial, when drank it will give you a powerful sword", then "(doesn't create items, that's conjuration...)". Is the vial a Crowsign thing, or the example that was ruled out?
  13. Recipe names: is a window line that says "Lesser Magic Wand" but makes an "Ember Sprite" acceptable, or should sprite crafts live in a menu with their own names?
  14. Level range: basic (level 60) can reach Enchanting 375, vanilla (40) 300, beta (20) 225. Which sprite tiers exist, and at what skill?
  15. Untested client behaviour the choice leans on. Does the client let Disenchant aim at a herb? Does a new item id show its icon everywhere? Does the client build the recipe window from its own files only (believed, not tried)?
